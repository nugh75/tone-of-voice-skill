# Tone of Voice

Use these rules as a style filter, not as permission to change the argument.

## Priority

1. The user's explicit instruction and approved conceptual architecture.
2. Accuracy of claims, evidence, citations, and construct boundaries.
3. Project style notes and applicable editorial norms.
4. The preferences below.

Surface genuine conflicts. Do not silently override an explicit user choice.

## Academic voice

- Prefer clear, concrete academic language. Preserve necessary theoretical and
  disciplinary terms; remove ornamental jargon only when it adds no precision.
- Develop each sentence as far as its reasoning requires. Use direct syntax
  where it improves clarity, but retain subordination when it expresses scope,
  conditions, causality, concession, or epistemic nuance.
- Use first person, impersonal forms, or explicit institutional agents according
  to disciplinary convention and local context. Prefer an identifiable agent
  over vague formulas such as `si ritiene` when responsibility matters.
- Define an acronym at its first relevant occurrence in the document. Re-expand
  it only when a long separation, a standalone excerpt, or editorial norms make
  that useful.
- Avoid redundant framing and accidental repetition. Repeat the same label for
  the same construct; never introduce synonyms merely to create variety.
- Use negative contrast (`non... ma...`) when it makes a conceptual boundary
  precise. Reduce it only when repetitive or rhetorically theatrical.
- Use connectors where the logical relation needs signalling. Initial, medial,
  and integrated positions are all valid; choose by rhythm and scope.
- Make pronouns, demonstratives, and omitted subjects unambiguous. Articles are
  not anaphoric pronouns; lexical repetition is preferable to an unclear
  antecedent.
- Prefer measured claims. Match causal and generalizing language to the study
  design and the available evidence.

## Conceptual and argumentative coherence

- Gloss a new concept at its first relevant occurrence and give its full
  treatment where the argument needs it. Cross-references may connect sections;
  thematic unity does not require isolating every concept in one section.
- Keep argument order intelligible, but allow previews and later development
  when they are explicitly signposted.
- State what is original and distinguish invention, adaptation, integration,
  and synthesis.
- In empirical passages, make constructs, methods, instruments, and analytic
  conditions clear before drawing broader interpretations.
- Discuss person, society, equity, ethics, or democratic responsibility where
  they are part of the research question or inference. Do not inject them as a
  mandatory formula into unrelated methodological or descriptive passages.

## Structural readability

- Give each paragraph one dominant argumentative function. Supporting
  functions may coexist only when they advance that function rather than open
  a competing line of reasoning.
- Make the opening sentence locate the paragraph's function in the surrounding
  argument; it need not mechanically announce a topic when the relation is
  already clear.
- Order sentences so that prerequisites precede dependent claims, support stays
  near the claim it supports, and implications follow the reasoning that
  warrants them.
- Use transitions to express a real relation such as elaboration, contrast,
  cause, concession, or consequence. Do not add connectors to conceal a missing
  logical bridge.
- Flag overloaded, orphaned, duplicated, or misplaced content. Route changes to
  the content architecture through `wayfinder` and `scrittura`; a style pass
  must not silently move, merge, cut, or invent substantive material.
- Let paragraph length follow argumentative function and readability. Do not
  enforce arbitrary sentence or word limits.

## Presentation

- Use metaphors or humour only when they clarify and fit the section; empirical
  and methodological passages normally benefit from plainer prose.
- Use lists when they expose a real set, sequence, or comparison more clearly
  than prose.
- Use parenthetical asides sparingly; move substantial digressions to notes or
  dedicated prose when the format supports them.
- Report exact values when exactness is supported and material. Preserve
  justified approximations and uncertainty.
- Prefer descriptive, concise titles; creative titles remain available when the
  user or publication context calls for them.

## Other skills

- `wayfinder` settles definitions, relations among ideas, and content
  architecture before stylistic rewriting.
- `scrittura` organizes source material, drafts, and compares formulations.
- `article-revision` governs approved file edits, citations, versioning, and
  optional Git publication. Style guidance alone never authorizes those actions.
